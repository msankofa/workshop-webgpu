Distance and masking

Every category attenuates on the WebAudio inverse model: gain = ref / (ref + rolloff x (clamp(d, ref, max) - ref)). This is where a callout that carries 55 m competes with gunfire that carries 90 m.

Voice source A/B

Takes are baked dry. The radio channel and the vocoder are runtime inserts, so one file plays over comms or out loud, as a human or as a machine. The vocoder is what carries per-bot identity for a sampled voice: the carrier is seeded per bot, so 90 bots sharing one file still do not sound like one throat. Bake more with node bake-voices.mjs --engine=eleven --voice=all.

Robot vocoder

A vocoder makes a machine voice out of a real one. It chops the take into frequency bands, measures how loud each band is moment to moment, and uses those levels to open and close the same bands of a synthetic buzz. The words and timing come from the take. The voice you actually hear is the buzz.

1. Bands — how finely it listens

Each bar is one band. Wider bars overlap and blur vowels together; narrow bars leave gaps that swallow parts of the sound.

2. Follower — how fast it reacts

Grey is the take's real loudness. Green is what the vocoder tracks. Too slow and consonants smear; too fast and the buzz leaks into the envelope.

3. Carrier — what the machine sounds like

A pure buzz has no way to make an "s". The noise is what gives it one.

4. Output

Grey is the take going in, green is the vocoded result. Big gaps mean the band settings are throwing that part of the voice away.

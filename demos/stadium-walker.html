Gait
Legs and balance

A planted foot that ends up further than this from where the gait believes it is has strayed. The threshold decides both what the sim acts on and what the readout counts, so the two can never disagree.

Measured across four species: at these defaults nothing strays, so the mode changes nothing. Break the gait — double the top speed — and only slow improves the sliding it is aimed at (Pikachu 48% to 30%, Ponyta 71% to 58%). Re-step and move the target both drive the stray count to nothing and make the sliding worse, because a foot parked at the reach limit gets dragged from there. See docs/subsystems/stadium.md.

A foot is a surface, not a point. Contact patch fits a rounded box to the foot bones and hulls the vertices that can reach the floor, then stands the creature on that ring instead of on one dot. The patch is anchored at the foothold the gait chose, so this changes the size of the support polygon and never where it sits.

Measured over six seeds each: two-legged creatures had no support polygon at all before, because two points hull to a line with no interior — Charizard 0% to 45% of frames with the centre of mass inside its support. Tauros 59% to 100%, Slowpoke 67% to 85%. Sliding and clamping are unchanged. Sandslash is unhelped: its foot bones are flat cards, so the patch falls back to the fitted box and comes out 0.6% of a leg wide.

Bone roles

The mapper guesses which bones are legs from geometry, and on some species it guesses wrong. Click a bone in the viewport with the skeleton shown, then give it a role. Roles are saved per species and applied whenever that species is spawned.

leg is an ordinary segment, knee is the one joint the two-bone solve bends at, foot is the sole and may be several bones, and attach is the body bone the limb hangs off rather than part of the limb.

How to read this

Dragging is a planted foot whose drawn position slides along the ground while the gait believes it is standing still. You see it as a foot skating, or as a leg that visibly cannot reach where its foot is supposed to be.

Tapping covers two different faults that look alike. One is a leg that lands, decides it wants to move again and lifts, cycling with nothing to show for it — that one has never been reproduced on these models, because a foot that just landed sits at zero error by construction and the trigger cannot re-fire. The other is a step that is simply over before it can be drawn. The foot is lerped along its arc with a half-sine lift, so a step lasting three frames is two interior samples, and the foot trail comes out as a triangular spike instead of an arc. That one is real and it is what you are usually seeing. Turn on foot trails and drop shortest step to watch it appear.

The pink stick is the gap between where the gait believes a foot is and where the solver could actually draw it. A stick you can see is the gait lying to itself.

The prediction is arithmetic on the tuning, so it answers the moment you move a slider. The measurement counts what the feet did. Checked against the shipped models it catches 83% of real dragging and cries wolf on 24% of what it flags, so read them together — a disagreement is called out above.

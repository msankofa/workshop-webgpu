# Adversary mode — what five independent designs agreed on (2026-08-11)

Five designers got the same brief ([`recon-digest.md`](recon-digest.md)), the same nine questions, and no
assigned angles — two on Fable, two on Opus, one on Sonnet. Identical prompts on purpose: agreement
between them is independent agreement, not a consequence of how I split the problem.

This document records where they converged, where they split, what I verified myself afterwards, and
what I recommend. **Nothing here has been built.**

---

## Corrections to the recon digest

Three of my facts were wrong. All three were caught by designers reading the source, and I have since
verified each myself.

**1. The 13 GB visibility field is not a live constraint.** Both viewers call
`buildLazyVisibilityField` (`nav-visibility.js:169`); every query is a DDA trace against a
direct-mapped memo, and memory is linear. The quadratic `buildVisibilityField` exists in the file and
**nothing calls it**. The viewer comment I quoted describes the eager path. See the measured table
below for what the real wall is.

**2. There is a top-down camera, and bot picking.** `environment-viewer-v2.html:11501` is a full
orbit/dev camera (drag orbit, WASD pan, scroll zoom), and `pickBotAtScreen` (`:6969`) already raycasts
against the ghost renderer's bot groups. My claim that an overhead commander needs a new camera and a
new selection framework was wrong — both exist.

**3. `bot-state-code.js` is wired into the game.** `environment-viewer-v2.html:7248` lazily imports it
behind `?botTrace=1`, with the 19-column TSV writer and legality checking. I said it was harness-only.

A fourth correction, to the encoder rather than the digest: its role slot is a **two-character
alphabet, `r` and `m`**. Sniper, technical, squad leader and drone operator all encode as `r`. Any
observation format built on those 9-char codes is blind to the role distinction that matters most to a
commander.

## The measured nav answer

I ran `bench-bot-nav.mjs`. Selected rows:

| span m | cell m | cells | walkable | grid MB | cold vis row ms | A\* ms | corner ms | **eager MB** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 384 | 1.5 | 65,536 | 34,232 | 0.6 | **6.7** | 1.06 | 7 | 140 |
| **512** | **2.0** | **65,536** | **31,896** | **0.6** | **4.5** | 1.01 | 11 | 121 |
| 512 | 1.5 | 116,964 | 56,967 | 1.1 | 11.1 | 1.04 | 11 | 387 |
| 1024 | 3.0 | 116,964 | 55,453 | 1.1 | 12.0 | 0.91 | 31 | 367 |
| 1200 | 1.5 | 640,000 | 329,828 | 6.1 | **301.6** | 1.16 | 52 | 12,969 |

The 13 GB is the last column, which is never built. **The real wall is the cold-visibility-row cost**,
and it is superlinear in walkable cells: 6.7 ms at 34k walkable, 301 ms at 330k. One bot taking cover
in an uncached cell on a 1200 m map would hitch the frame by a third of a second.

So the binding constraint is **walkable cell count, not map size** — and it binds at roughly 65,000
cells however many metres they cover.

That yields the key result: **512 m at 2.0 m pitch costs exactly what the game already pays at 384 m
at 1.5 m** — same 65,536 cells, slightly *cheaper* cold row, same 0.6 MB — for 1.8× the battlefield.

And the whole-map static path already exists. `environment-viewer-v2.html:2334` bakes the entire map
when a bounded layout is loaded, and `botNavGridCovers` returns `true` unconditionally when
`botNavZone` is null (`:2401`, commented "the grid IS the map"). A bounded arena gets full-map
navigation today, with no anchor, no rebake, no drift, and no local-window fallback.

## Where all five converged

Independent agreement, five for five, unless noted.

| Question | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Embodiment** | The commander has a body and fights on foot. **5/5.** |
| **Overhead RTS camera** | Do not build one. **5/5**, including the two designers who found the orbit camera and still rejected it. |
| **Tactical layer** | The existing fullscreen `M` map (`world-map.js`), extended with base rings, squad blips and a click handler. **5/5.** |
| **Income** | Generated by held territory, not by kills. **5/5** (one adds a small kill bounty on top). |
| **Bot respawn** | **Must be turned off.** Free respawn makes a purchased body worthless and the economy decorative. **5/5.** |
| **Capture** | Built on `bot-spawn-markers.js`, driven by presence inside the existing radius. **5/5.** |
| **Contested bases** | Progress **freezes** while both sides are present — never a tug-of-war, because tug-of-war rewards trickling bodies in from safety instead of winning the firefight. **5/5.** |
| **Orders** | Move off the bot id onto the **squad record**. This fixes the verified dead-commander bug as a side effect. **5/5.** |
| **Attack order** | Exists, but **never assigns a target**. It is a destination and a weighting. **5/5.** |
| **New FSM rungs** | **Zero.** Orders write the out-of-combat movement branch and the two existing toggle biases, nothing else. **5/5.** |
| **The immune states** | Flee, heal, knife, committed cover, close-self-threat and medic duty stay immune to orders. **5/5**, several calling it the thing that makes these read as soldiers rather than cursors. |
| **Match end** | Three doors, one of which must be a **timeout** — otherwise a machine opponent plays forever. **5/5.** |
| **Command API** | One JSON command message over the existing `mp:guest_input` channel; **the relay needs no changes at all**. **5/5.** |
| **Observation** | A separate, much slower, squad-level message — not per-frame, not per-bot. **5/5.** |
| **LLM cadence** | One decision every 5–15 s, because nothing a commander decides resolves faster than that. **5/5.** Cost estimates cluster at 200–250k tokens per match. |
| **Build order** | Pure Node-tested modules → harness → game → multiplayer → LLM last. **5/5.** |

### The single strongest argument produced

Against a bodiless commander, and it is not about UI. `botZoneAnchor` (`environment-viewer-v2.html:2476`)
reads the local player's pose, and with no body falls through to
`for (const rec of botPlayers.values()) return botXZ(rec.bot)` — **the first entry in the map, meaning
an arbitrary bot in insertion order.** A commander without a body parks the one persistent nav grid on
whichever bot happens to be oldest, permanently degrading every fight that doesn't contain it, with no
player control over which. Verified.

Bounding the map (below) would dissolve this particular argument, since a static grid needs no anchor.
The other four reasons for a body stand regardless: the FPS combat is the repo's best asset, the
procedural rigs and wound modelling are authored for eye height, "I control one team" reads as
participation, and an embodied commander gives all three opponent types the same command vocabulary.

## Where they split

**Navigation.** Four of five bound the map. Three proposed a single static whole-map bake; one proposed
two moving bubbles plus a reduced-fidelity mode outside them; one proposed two bubbles plus abstracted
statistical resolution for off-screen fights.

The split tracks my error precisely. The designer who proposed abstracted resolution is the one who
took the 13 GB figure at face value and engineered around a cost that does not exist. **Four of five
independently rejected abstracted off-screen combat** on the grounds that it forks the game into two
truths — a resolver produces deaths with no weapon, no wound, no voice line, no corpse and no dropped
packs — and that in a competitive mode the two sides' fights would resolve by different rules depending
on where the camera is, which makes the result non-comparable.

**Buy granularity.** Three buy individual bots by role; one buys pre-formed squads, arguing that
`planSquadReconcile`'s absorb pass will fold loose purchases into squads you did not design; one offers
both.

**Build delay.** Four impose a 5–6 s per-bot build queue. One argues the walk from base to front is
already a 60–100 s timer and stacking a second wait produces no interesting decision.

**Population.** One ties the population cap to territory, making money you cannot spend the pressure
that forces you onto the map. The others cap population flat or not at all.

## What I recommend

**Bound the arena to 512 m and bake it once at 2.0 m.** It is measured, it costs exactly what the game
already pays, and it deletes the anchor, the rebake, the cover-corner remapping, the local-window
fallback, and the off-zone flee freeze — where `findFleeGoal` calls `floodFill` on a grid that does not
cover the bot, gets null, and the bot simply stops moving. Every tactical system then works everywhere,
for every bot, always. The price is that adversary mode does not run on 1200 m maps or infinite
procedural terrain, and is flat until terrain relief is added later.

**Put the commander in a body.** Five for five, and the arguments are about the codebase rather than
about taste.

**Take the order model as the consensus describes it**: six or so squad-scoped orders on the squad
record, delivered to the leader so the existing formation system carries the rest, with zero new FSM
rungs and `attack` implemented as a bias term inside `selectBotTarget`'s existing risk product.

**Build the scripted opponent before the LLM one.** Four of five said so independently: the script is
the test harness for the entire command channel, and it is also what makes the mode playable at all.
The LLM seat is the headline and the least load-bearing — it changes no game code, because it is just
another guest on the relay.

**Ship the readable event log with the first playable phase, not as polish.** The risk every designer
named, in different words, is the same one: a commander who orders squads he cannot see, watches them
die as blips, and learns nothing. Two independently concluded that the compliance readout — showing
*why* a squad is not obeying, because it is pinned or fleeing rather than deaf — is what decides
whether orders feel like they work.

## Open decisions

These are genuinely the owner's, and the swarm did not resolve them:

1. **Buy bots or buy squads.** Squad-buying has the better technical argument; bot-buying has the finer
   control.
2. **Build queue or no build queue.** Whether the walk alone is enough of a timer.
3. **Population tied to territory, or flat.** Territory-linked population is the most interesting
   proposal in the set and also the biggest snowball risk.
4. **Arena size.** 512 m is what the measurement supports. Larger is possible at coarser pitch — 1024 m
   at 3.0 m is 117k cells and a 12 ms cold row, roughly double today's cost.
